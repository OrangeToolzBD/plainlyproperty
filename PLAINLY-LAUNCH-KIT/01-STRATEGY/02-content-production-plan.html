Research First,
Then Write in Bulk

Thirteen pages produced by a two-stage pipeline: a research pass that returns only sourced facts, then a writing pass that can only use those facts. Copy-paste prompts and a runnable script.

The separation is the whole design. A model asked to research and write in one step will fill gaps with plausible numbers. A model given a fixed pack and told it may use nothing else, cannot.

01 — Why Two Stages

The failure this design prevents

The problem

Ask any model to "write a page about snagging costs in Dubai" and it will produce a confident price range. Some of it will be real. You will not be able to tell which parts.

The fix

Stage 1 returns facts with source URLs and writes NOT FOUND where it cannot source something. Stage 2 receives that pack and is told plainly: if a figure is not in the pack, it does not go on the page.

What you get

Pages that carry [NEEDS SOURCE] in two places instead of pages that carry three invented prices. The first is publishable after one phone call. The second is a liability.

Why this matters more in Dubai than elsewhere. Publishing a wrong fee on a page that also carries a broker's BRN is not just an accuracy problem — it is a regulated firm publishing incorrect information about a regulated transaction. The NOT FOUND discipline is a compliance control, not editorial fussiness.

03 — Stage 1: The Research Prompt

Runs on Perplexity, with Brave supplying primary-source URLs

One call per page. Perplexity does the reasoning and citation; Brave finds the operator and authority pages Perplexity may have summarised second-hand. Save as prompt_research.txt.

You are researching ONE page for a Dubai property website. Return FACTS ONLY,
each with the source URL beside it. You are not writing the page.

PAGE:            {slug}
TARGET KEYWORD:  {primary}  ({vol} searches/month, AED {cpc} per click)
PAGE FORMAT:     {fmt}
WHY IT EXISTS:   {role_note}

FIND AND RETURN, each as a separate line with its source URL:

1. PRICES. Every published price relevant to this page, from named Dubai operators or named
   government schedules. Give the figure, the unit, the operator or authority, and the date it
   was published. Minimum 6 separate priced data points. If a company hides its price behind a
   contact form, record that as "not published" against its name - that absence is itself
   useful, because publishing the number is how this page wins the click.

2. THE RULE. The governing regulation, permit or law, quoted verbatim, with the article or
   section number and the issuing authority (DLD, RERA, DET, GDRFA, ICP, UAE Civil Code).
   Never paraphrase a regulation. Quote it.

3. TIMINGS. Real processing times, deadlines and validity windows, from the authority's own
   page - not from a blog.

4. THE NUMBERS BEHIND THE ADVICE. Whatever quantitative backing this page needs: occupancy
   rates, service charge per square foot, achieved yields, LTV caps, fee percentages. Named
   source, dated.

5. WHAT THE INCUMBENTS OMIT. Open the top 5 ranking pages for the target keyword. List the
   questions each one leaves unanswered. This is the brief for the section that beats them.

6. THE COUNTER-EXAMPLE. One documented case where the standard advice is wrong, or a cost
   people are commonly surprised by. Sourced.

RULES:
- If you cannot source something, write NOT FOUND. Do not estimate, do not infer, do not fill
  a gap with a plausible number. A page with three honest gaps is publishable; a page with one
  invented figure is not.
- Prefer the primary source. A search snippet saying a fee is AED 750 is not a source for
  AED 750 - open the operator's own page or the authority's own schedule.
- Flag anything older than 18 months as STALE.
- Dubai-specific only. Abu Dhabi and Sharjah rules differ and must not be mixed in.

Return as JSON: {{"prices":[...],"rule":{{...}},"timings":[...],"numbers":[...],
"gaps_in_top5":[...],"counter_example":{{...}},"not_found":[...],"stale":[...]}}

One API detail that will cost you an hour if you miss it. Brave's country parameter does not accept AE — it returns HTTP 422 with an enum error, and if your code swallows exceptions you will get zero results and assume the market is empty. Omit the parameter and carry the geography in the query text instead. The runner below already does this.

04 — Stage 2: The Writing Prompt

Runs on the Anthropic Batches API, one request per page

The research pack is injected wholesale at {research_json}. Save as prompt_write.txt. Placeholders in braces are filled by the runner from pages_final.json.

You are writing one page for a Dubai property website owned by a licensed
RERA broker. Write the page body only. No preamble, no explanation, no meta-commentary.

=== THE PAGE ===
URL:        {slug}
TITLE TAG:  {title}
META:       {meta}
FORMAT:     {fmt}
WORD COUNT: 900-1,300 words of prose. Tables do not count toward it.

=== TARGET KEYWORDS (from measured search data - do not invent others) ===
{kwblock}

=== RESEARCH PACK - EVERY FIGURE YOU MAY USE ===
{research_json}

=== HOW TO WRITE IT ===

1. OPEN WITH THE ANSWER. First 40-60 words answer the exact question in the target keyword,
   with a number in it. This is what gets quoted in an AI Overview and it is what stops the
   reader going back to the results page. No throat-clearing, no "in today's market".

2. PUT THE PRICE IN EARLY AND IN PUBLIC. Every competitor for these terms hides pricing behind
   a contact form. Publishing a real range is the single highest-leverage thing on the page.
   Use a table. Where you only have a range, say it is a range and say what moves it.

3. HEADINGS ARE THE QUESTIONS PEOPLE TYPE. Use the target keywords as H2s where they read as
   real questions. Never stack keywords into a heading that a human would not say aloud.

4. QUOTE THE REGULATION VERBATIM. Where the research pack contains a rule, quote it with its
   article number and authority. Do not paraphrase law.

5. EVERY NUMBER TRACES TO THE PACK. If a figure is not in the research pack above, it does not
   go on the page. If the pack says NOT FOUND for something the page needs, write
   "[NEEDS SOURCE]" inline and continue. Do not estimate.

6. ONE CROSS-SELL, PLACED LATE. Near the end, one sentence linking to /property-valuation-dubai/
   phrased as the question this reader is already asking. Examples of the register:
   "If you are weighing whether to re-let or sell, a current valuation is the first number you
   need." Never a banner, never a hard sell, never more than one.

=== HARD PROHIBITIONS ===
- NEVER list a specific property with a price, a unit number or an address. Dubai requires a
  Trakheesi permit plus BRN and ORN on any property advertisement; fines start at AED 50,000.
  This page publishes advice, data and tools - it is not a property advertisement, and it must
  stay that way.
- NEVER promise or project a return, a yield or price appreciation. Not "expect 8%",
  not "prices are set to rise". The market cooled in 2026; H1 residential transactions fell
  13.6%. Publishing an appreciation promise is both a compliance problem and wrong.
- NEVER claim to be the cheapest, the best or number one.
- NEVER invent a company name, a phone number, a fee or an opening hour.
- Do not reuse a sentence from any other page on this site.

=== OUTPUT ===
Markdown. One H1 matching the title intent. H2s as above. Tables as real markdown tables.
End with a "Last reviewed" line carrying today's date. Then, after a line containing only
`---SCHEMA---`, output the FAQPage JSON-LD for the questions this page answers, with answer
text copied verbatim from the page body.

05 — The Runner

Three commands, start to finished pages
python run_content.py --stage research     # 13 Perplexity + 39 Brave calls
python run_content.py --stage write        # submits one Anthropic batch
python run_content.py --stage collect      # polls, then writes pages/*.md

Save as run_content.py alongside pages_final.json. Needs PERPLEXITY_API_KEY, BRAVE_API_KEY and ANTHROPIC_API_KEY in the environment.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Two-stage content pipeline: research -> inject -> write.

Stage 1 runs per page against Perplexity and Brave, and writes a research pack to disk.
Stage 2 submits every page to the Anthropic Message Batches API with its pack injected.
Batches is roughly half the per-token cost of single calls and returns within 24h; for 13
pages it typically lands in minutes.

    python run_content.py --stage research      # ~13 API calls, few minutes
    python run_content.py --stage write         # submits one batch
    python run_content.py --stage collect       # polls and writes pages/*.md
"""
import os, sys, json, time, argparse, pathlib, requests

ROOT = pathlib.Path(__file__).parent
PAGES = json.loads((ROOT / 'pages_final.json').read_text(encoding='utf-8'))
(ROOT / 'research').mkdir(exist_ok=True)
(ROOT / 'pages').mkdir(exist_ok=True)

PPLX = os.environ['PERPLEXITY_API_KEY']
BRAVE = os.environ['BRAVE_API_KEY']
ANTHROPIC = os.environ['ANTHROPIC_API_KEY']
MODEL = 'claude-opus-4-6'          # set to the model you intend to bill

RESEARCH_PROMPT = open(ROOT / 'prompt_research.txt', encoding='utf-8').read()
WRITE_PROMPT = open(ROOT / 'prompt_write.txt', encoding='utf-8').read()


def brave(q, n=12):
    """Brave finds the primary source. It is never the citation itself."""
    r = requests.get('https://api.search.brave.com/res/v1/web/search',
                     headers={'X-Subscription-Token': BRAVE, 'Accept': 'application/json'},
                     params={'q': q, 'count': n}, timeout=30)
    # NOTE: Brave rejects country=AE with HTTP 422 - the enum does not include it.
    # Carry the geo in the query text instead.
    if r.status_code != 200:
        return []
    return [(x.get('title', ''), x.get('url', ''), x.get('description', ''))
            for x in r.json().get('web', {}).get('results', [])]


def perplexity(prompt):
    r = requests.post('https://api.perplexity.ai/chat/completions',
        headers={'Authorization': 'Bearer ' + PPLX, 'Content-Type': 'application/json'},
        json={'model': 'sonar-pro',
              'messages': [{'role': 'user', 'content': prompt}],
              'temperature': 0.1}, timeout=180)
    r.raise_for_status()
    j = r.json()
    return dict(text=j['choices'][0]['message']['content'],
                citations=j.get('citations', []))


def stage_research():
    for p in PAGES:
        out = ROOT / 'research' / ('%02d.json' % p['no'])
        if out.exists():
            print('skip', p['slug']); continue
        primary = p['kws'][0]['kw'] if p['kws'] else p['slug'].strip('/').replace('-', ' ')
        prompt = RESEARCH_PROMPT.format(
            slug=p['slug'], primary=primary,
            vol=p['vol'], cpc=(p['kws'][0]['cpc_aed'] if p['kws'] else 0),
            fmt=p['fmt'], role_note=p['role'])
        res = perplexity(prompt)
        # Brave supplies the primary-source URLs Perplexity may have summarised
        res['brave'] = {q: brave(q) for q in
                        [primary + ' dubai price', primary + ' dubai official',
                         primary + ' dld rera rule']}
        out.write_text(json.dumps(res, indent=1), encoding='utf-8')
        print('researched', p['slug'])
        time.sleep(1)


def stage_write():
    reqs = []
    for p in PAGES:
        pack = (ROOT / 'research' / ('%02d.json' % p['no']))
        if not pack.exists():
            print('NO RESEARCH PACK - skipping', p['slug']); continue
        kwblock = '\n'.join('  %-46s %5d/mo   AED %6.2f' % (k['kw'], k['vol'], k['cpc_aed'])
                             for k in p['kws']) or '  (support page - no head term)'
        body = WRITE_PROMPT.format(slug=p['slug'], title=p['title'], meta=p['meta'],
                                   fmt=p['fmt'], kwblock=kwblock,
                                   research_json=pack.read_text(encoding='utf-8')[:60000])
        reqs.append({'custom_id': 'page-%02d' % p['no'],
                     'params': {'model': MODEL, 'max_tokens': 8000,
                                'messages': [{'role': 'user', 'content': body}]}})
    r = requests.post('https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages/batches',
        headers={'x-api-key': ANTHROPIC, 'anthropic-version': '2023-06-01',
                 'content-type': 'application/json'},
        json={'requests': reqs}, timeout=120)
    r.raise_for_status()
    bid = r.json()['id']
    (ROOT / 'batch_id.txt').write_text(bid)
    print('submitted batch', bid, 'with', len(reqs), 'pages')


def stage_collect():
    bid = (ROOT / 'batch_id.txt').read_text().strip()
    h = {'x-api-key': ANTHROPIC, 'anthropic-version': '2023-06-01'}
    while True:
        j = requests.get('https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages/batches/' + bid,
                         headers=h, timeout=60).json()
        if j['processing_status'] == 'ended':
            break
        print('status', j['processing_status'], j.get('request_counts')); time.sleep(30)
    res = requests.get(j['results_url'], headers=h, timeout=300)
    by_no = {'page-%02d' % p['no']: p for p in PAGES}
    for line in res.text.splitlines():
        if not line.strip(): continue
        row = json.loads(line)
        p = by_no[row['custom_id']]
        if row['result']['type'] != 'succeeded':
            print('FAILED', p['slug'], row['result']); continue
        text = row['result']['message']['content'][0]['text']
        name = p['slug'].strip('/').replace('/', '-') or 'home'
        (ROOT / 'pages' / (name + '.md')).write_text(text, encoding='utf-8')
        print('wrote', name + '.md', len(text.split()), 'words')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument('--stage', required=True, choices=['research', 'write', 'collect'])
    a = ap.parse_args()
    {'research': stage_research, 'write': stage_write, 'collect': stage_collect}[a.stage]()

Why Batches rather than 13 single calls. The Batches API is roughly half the per-token price and is designed for exactly this shape of job — many independent, self-contained requests with no dependency between them. Research and quality-checking stay outside the batch, because those need judgement; only the writing step is mechanical enough to batch.

06 — Before You Publish

The check that decides whether this was worth doing
1

Grep every page for [NEEDS SOURCE]

Each one is a phone call, not a blocker. Make the calls, fill the gaps, and keep a note of which authority answered. That note becomes the citation.

2

Check every figure against the pack

Automated: pull every number from the page, confirm it appears in the research JSON. Anything that does not is either a hallucination or an arithmetic step that needs showing.

3

Confirm no page names a property

Search all 13 for a unit number, a building plus a price, or an address plus a price. Any hit is a Trakheesi exposure and must be rewritten before launch.

4

Confirm no page promises a return

Search for "expect", "projected", "guaranteed", "will rise", "%% ROI". The market fell 13.6% in H1 2026 — these claims are both non-compliant and wrong.

5

Check cross-page duplication

No two pages should share more than 25% of their body text. Thirteen pages from one prompt template will drift toward each other unless this is measured.

6

Confirm every page links once to the valuation page

Exactly once, late, phrased as a question. This is the only thing turning traffic into commission — and it is the easiest instruction for a batch job to quietly drop.

07 — Revised Production Order

What changed after the plan was checked

Do not run all thirteen pages first. A council review of this plan flagged the thirteen-page launch as undeliverable for a solo operator: one person cannot build thirteen pages, tune a paid campaign, and call valuation enquiries back inside fifteen minutes. The pages get built and the leads go cold — which breaks the exact conversion assumption the whole forecast rests on.

Batch 1 — one page

Run the pipeline for page 1 only (/property-valuation-dubai/). It is the page the paid campaign points at and the only one with a revenue path this quarter. One research call, one write call. Publish, then start advertising.

Batch 2 — the snagging cluster

Pages 4, 5 and 6, once valuation is live and the campaign is running. Lowest organic barrier on the board at 79 referring domains, and the highest click prices in the plan.

Batch 3 — everything else

The remaining nine pages, from month 3. By then the measured click-to-enquiry rate is known and the page briefs can be tuned against real behaviour rather than assumptions.

Run the pipeline on one page before you batch anything. Submit page 1 alone, read the output end to end, and check every figure against its research pack by hand. A prompt fault found on page one costs one call; the same fault found after a thirteen-page batch costs the batch and the reading time to spot it.

08 — The Keyword Hunt That Came Back Empty

Why the page list did not grow

A deeper hunt was run specifically to find fast-ranking, high-value long-tail that would let content earn sooner. It is recorded here because the negative result changes how these prompts should be used.

  • 401 community-level long-tails were priced — every service crossed with 27 Dubai communities, plus 20 question-shaped queries. 98% returned zero measurable volume. There is no "snagging inspection jvc" seam to write into.
  • The one apparent gem was a mirage. Off-plan buying terms show an ad-competition index of 0.10 at AED 41 a click — but the microsites holding page one carry a median of 747 referring domains. Ad competition is not organic difficulty, and in this market they invert: the term with the heaviest ad competition (snagging, 0.86) sits behind the lowest organic barrier (79).

What this means for the writing prompts. Those sub-ten-searches-a-month phrasings are still worth covering — as sections and FAQ entries inside the thirteen pages, not as pages of their own. The research prompt already asks for the questions incumbents leave unanswered; that output is where this long-tail belongs. Thirteen deep pages will out-rank forty thin ones here, because the market is not big enough to support forty.
